# Hand pilot 2, 2026-11-17: six more invasive species, and what goes wrong

By MV2 of Munim, Inc. (mv2@munimtech.com). Made with AI agents working in his name.
Licence: CC BY 4.0. GBIF data cited by occurrence key; observer names are not stored.

**This is not a pre-registered study.** IA-1 (PREREGISTRATION.md) is still queued and IA-2
(IA2-PREREG.md) has not had its baseline run, so neither has any output. Like pilot 1
(PILOT-2026-11-17.md), this is a hand-picked, biased check of the raw-candidate rule. It
was not used to change any IA-1 or IA-2 definition, threshold or hypothesis.

## Method

Same as pilot 1, with every query pinned to the legacy GBIF Backbone
(`checklistKey=d7dddbf4-2cf0-4f39-9b2a-bb099caae36c`). We compared the country facets
for `year=2025,2026` and `year=1000,2024`. Countries with records only in 2025-2026 are
raw candidates. We read every record of each candidate, then the source record (the
iNaturalist or Artportalen page), and searched for prior reports: EPPO Global Database
distribution and reporting pages, national pages and the literature. Read on
2026-11-17, about 14:45-15:30 UTC. The classes are the IA-1 classes in their fixed order:
native, already reported, not wild, doubtful, plausible.

Species: *Solenopsis invicta* (5035230), *Aromia bungii* (5001658), *Xylotrechus
chinensis* (8325707), *Spodoptera frugiperda* (5109855), *Procambarus virginalis*
(9442269), *Aedes albopictus* (1651430). A repeat of *Lycorma delicatula* gave the same
two candidates as pilot 1 (Hungary and Puerto Rico) and is not counted again.

## What pilot 2 teaches (before any IA-1 or IA-2 output)

1. **Photo identification is the main failure.** Four of the five doubtful pairs are
   species with a common look-alike: *S. invicta* vs the red form of *S. geminata*,
   *Ae. albopictus* vs *Ae. aegypti*, and *A. bungii* vs *A. moschata*. They became
   research grade on the observer's computer-vision suggestion plus one agreement.
2. **A validator's verdict may never reach GBIF.** The Swedish *A. bungii* record was
   rejected on Artportalen 19 days before we read it, yet GBIF still serves it under
   the quarantine name. So the checker must read the source page, not only GBIF.
3. **Official services do act on citizen records, and can find nothing.** Morocco's NPPO
   surveyed the Casablanca site within about a month of the iNaturalist record and found
   no beetles, so EPPO lists it as an unreliable record. Pilot 1's Polish hornet case
   started from an iNaturalist photo, too. A strong record is not a population: the
   watch needs an outcome label for "surveyed, not found", which is not a confirmation.
4. **The native-range check is not optional.** GRIIS lists *S. invicta* as invasive in
   Bolivia, yet the species is native next door in southeastern Peru, so a new-country
   flag can sit inside the native range.

## Worth a specialist's look (not alerts)

The three *Ae. albopictus* photos from Abu Dhabi, Dubai and Al Rayyan
(iNaturalist 340987467, 347526073 and 258016438). We could not find a published record
of the Asian tiger mosquito in the UAE or Qatar. If any of the three is real, it matters
for dengue and chikungunya control in the Gulf. The far likelier explanation is
*Ae. aegypti*, which is established in both cities. We class all three as doubtful and
contact nobody. These records are public, so any entomologist can review them on
iNaturalist.
