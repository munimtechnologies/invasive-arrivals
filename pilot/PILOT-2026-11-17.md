# Hand pilot, 2026-11-17: does the screen's rule find real arrivals?

By MV2 of Munim, Inc. (mv2@munimtech.com). Made with AI agents working in his name.
Licence: CC BY 4.0. GBIF data cited by occurrence key; observer names are not stored.

**This is not the pre-registered study.** The pre-registered screen (IA-1, see
PREREGISTRATION.md) is queued and has produced no output yet. This pilot applied the
same raw-candidate rule by hand, through the public GBIF API, to **six famous invasive
insects picked by us**. That makes it a biased, hand-picked check of whether the rule has
any signal at all. It was not used to change any IA-1 definition or threshold.

## Method

For each species we compared the GBIF country facet for all time with the facet for
2025-2026 (`occurrence/search?scientificName=<name>&facet=country&facetLimit=400&limit=0`
with and without `&year=2025,2026`, read 2026-11-17 about 10:50-11:30 UTC). A country is a
raw candidate when every GBIF record there is from 2025-2026. We read every record of each
candidate pair. Then we searched the web and the EPPO Reporting Service for an earlier
official or published report, and assigned the IA-1 verification classes in their fixed
order (native, already reported, not wild, doubtful, plausible). We skipped the GRIIS
"already listed" filter (IA-1 applies it), so a pilot pair may be one IA-1 drops.

Species checked: *Lycorma delicatula*, *Popillia japonica*, *Vespa velutina*,
*Aethina tumida*, *Agrilus planipennis*, *Anoplophora glabripennis*.
*A. planipennis* and *A. glabripennis* produced no raw candidates.

## What the pilot teaches (before IA-1 grades anything)

1. **The rule has real signal on high-impact pests.** For three of the most-watched
   insect invaders in Europe (spotted lanternfly, Japanese beetle, Asian hornet), the 2025-2026
   first national records show up as raw candidates.
2. **Event date is not discovery date.** The Hungarian naturgucker record is dated
   2026-07-01, but its GBIF key (6890326257) is among the newest, so it was probably indexed
   after the official announcement. A screen only "sees" a record once GBIF has it. Honest
   lead times therefore need a **prospective, weekly** run that logs when each pair first
   appeared. A retrospective run can only give an upper bound.
3. **Photo or specimen evidence matters.** Every weak (no-photo) pair we read was either
   already known (Belgium) or unconvincing (Greece, Albania). The strong filter is doing its job.
4. **GBIF lacks most official records.** Japanese beetle incursions in Austria, France and
   Croatia (EPPO 2025-2026) have no GBIF records at all. So "absent from GBIF" never means
   "unreported", and the hand check against the literature is essential.
