**This is not a pre-registered study.** IA-1 (PREREGISTRATION.md) and the IA-2 baseline
(IA2-PREREG.md) are still queued, so neither has any output. Like pilots 1 and 2, this is a
hand-picked, biased check of the raw-candidate rule, and nothing here changes any IA-1 or
IA-2 definition, threshold or hypothesis. Pilots 1 and 2 used insects, a crayfish and a
mosquito. This pilot asks whether the rule behaves differently for **plants**, which make up
most of the GRIIS lists.

## Method

This is the same as pilot 2. Every query is pinned to the legacy GBIF Backbone
(`checklistKey=d7dddbf4-2cf0-4f39-9b2a-bb099caae36c`). We compared the country facets for
`year=2025,2026` and `year=1000,2024`, then read every record of each raw candidate and its
iNaturalist page: the identifications, whether computer vision was used, and the comments.
Then we searched for prior reports: EPPO Global Database distribution pages, national lists,
CABI, Flora of China and the literature. Read 2026-11-17, about 16:50-17:40 UTC. The classes
are the IA-1 classes in their fixed order: native, already reported, not wild, doubtful,
plausible. The class-4 labels are from IA2-ADDENDUM-1.

Species (backbone key): *Parthenium hysterophorus* (3086784), *Mikania micrantha* (5398421),
*Solanum elaeagnifolium* (2929892), *Humulus scandens* (2984537), *Microstegium vimineum*
(5289808), *Pueraria montana* (2977636).

## What pilot 3 teaches (before any IA-1 or IA-2 output)

1. **For plants, the main failure is old reports that never reached GBIF, not photo
   look-alikes.** Vietnam has listed *M. micrantha* as invasive by law since 2011, yet
   under the pinned backbone GBIF had no Vietnamese record of it before 2025. Laos is
   similar. Four of the nine comparable strong pairs were "already reported" in national
   lists or reports that are not GBIF data. A checker must search national legal lists and
   grey literature, not only EPPO and papers.
2. **The native range shows up again (2 of 9).** Both Macao pairs are East Asian species
   at home in southern China. GRIIS lists them as invasive elsewhere, so a "new country"
   can sit inside the native range. IA2-ADDENDUM-1's rule to check POWO or Flora of China
   is needed for plants too.
3. **One look-alike case, and it is caught by an official verdict.** EPPO rejects every
   African *M. micrantha* record, so the Cameroon pair is doubtful before anyone looks at
   the photo. An official "invalid record" status is a cheap automatic filter worth
   reporting next to the W2 split.
4. **Undated records: the registered code handles them, and our hand method did not.**
   GBIF holds undated TAXREF checklist records of kudzu for French Guiana. They fall outside
   both year ranges used in these hand pilots, so the pair looked new by hand. watch.py
   classes any pair with an undated record as `historical`, and IA-1's arrivals.py compares
   against the all-time country facet, which includes undated records. Neither would have
   flagged it. Pilots 1 and 2 used the same year-range method and should be read with this
   in mind.
5. **Expert identifiers appear on plant records.** In the two strongest Parthenium pairs
   (UAE, DRC), the second ID was made without computer vision by an identifier who works on
   that region's flora or that weed. This feeds the descriptive split in IA2-ADDENDUM-1 §5.

## Worth a specialist's look (not alerts)

- ***Parthenium hysterophorus* in Bukavu, DR Congo** (iNaturalist 300129994, 2025-07-22).
  If confirmed, this would be the first published record of parthenium weed in the DRC. It
  is one of the world's worst agricultural and health weeds (crop losses, dermatitis,
  asthma). CABI has named the DRC as a country at risk that has not yet been invaded. The
  record has four photos and an independent agreeing identification. Bukavu borders
  Rwanda's Rusizi district, so spread across the border is a simple explanation.
- ***Solanum elaeagnifolium* in Syunik, Armenia** (iNaturalist 312889886, 2025-09-10).
  This EPPO A2 quarantine weed had its first Caucasus record in Azerbaijan in 2016. We
  found no record from Armenia. There is one photo, with a computer-vision ID and one
  agreement, so it needs a botanist's look.

We contact nobody about these, and never the observers. Both records are public, so any
specialist can review them on iNaturalist. Under IA2-ADDENDUM-1 they would get a
later_outcome on 2027-06-30.
